# Conventions and shared context for the service PRDs

Version 1.1, 7 Oct 2026: version 1 (6 Oct 2026) with the decisions of D2 folded in. Every rule that changed carries the ADR it comes from, written "(ADR-nnnn)"; the ADR in `docs/decisions/` holds the full rule, the options weighed and the reasons. Where a PRD line disagrees with this document or with an ADR, the ADR wins (ADR-0001). Questions still open are in `docs/decisions/DEFERRED.md`, each with the session or person who settles it.

The contract is these documents and what F2, F3 and F8 build from them: the ADRs; this file; F2's `packages/contracts` and `docs/contracts/VERSIONING.md`; F3's `supabase/migrations` and `docs/contracts/TABLE-OWNERS.md`; F8's `clickhouse/migrations`. `docs/contracts/INVENTORY.md` and `CONFLICTS.md` are D1's record of the PRDs as written, background only (ADR-0070).

Product: a social-media and news listening platform for the Iraqi market, built by Abdullah's team (stack: Supabase Postgres, n8n, Node, React, FlutterFlow; plus Redpanda, ClickHouse, Hetzner Object Storage with an off-site copy of the raw archive on Backblaze B2 (ADR-0027), Hetzner servers). Clients are brands, companies and potentially Iraqi government bodies (civilian ministries and state companies). Volume target at full scale: about 1,000,000 new posts and comments a day (30.5M a month): Facebook 12.0M, TikTok 7.5M, Instagram 4.5M, YouTube 3.0M, Telegram 2.4M, X 0.6M, LinkedIn 0.15M, news 0.375M articles, plus 60,000 web-search queries a month. Retention target: stored items ten years wherever a platform's terms, a vendor contract and copyright allow, otherwise their limit; item-level derived rows (analysis scores, hits, metrics time series) follow their item's class; aggregates and rollups ten years, except rollups of one YouTube channel, which keep 36 months (ADR-0054, ADR-0056).

n8n carries every notice and decision card to people (email, Telegram, Slack): services call it through one SDK client with one signing scheme and two payload schemas, `n8n.alert/v1` for one-way notices and `n8n.card/v1` for decision requests with buttons, a default action and a timeout; every answer, a button press or an ops request, comes back only through the audited admin API, which hands it to the owning service. D3 specifies the flows (ADR-0067).

Two hard constraints decided on 6 Oct 2026:
1. No Israeli-owned or Israel-affiliated vendor anywhere in the stack. Excluded: Bright Data, NetNut/Alarum, Nimble, Tavily, Webz.io. Flagged (client decides): Cloudflare, ClickHouse Inc. (open-source server self-hosted is fine), Perplexity (minor), Yandex. Cleared vendors used below: Oxylabs/Decodo, Apify (Czechia), EnsembleData (Singapore), Telemetrio (Estonia), DataForSEO (Estonia), Mojeek (UK), Perplexity (US, minor flag), Hetzner, Backblaze, Meilisearch, Typesense, Redpanda, Supabase, Hugging Face. Weakly cleared (country known, owner not verified): ScrapeCreators, SociaVault, HikerAPI, TikHub, twitterapi.io, DataImpulse, Scrapfly. Apify Actor publishers, not yet verified: harvestapi, tugelbay, sovereigntaylor. The user, or counsel for the user, verifies the owner and country of each weakly cleared vendor and Actor publisher before its probe and at each renewal, records it in `docs/dependencies.md` and this list, and until then its flag value cannot be set. A client may decline a flagged vendor (`clients.accepted_flagged_vendors`) (ADR-0051, ADR-0043).
2. No route that rests on a breach of a platform's terms. Route classes: GREEN = official API or licensed index under a contract we hold. AMBER = data bought from a screened vendor that does the collection itself; the breach, if any, sits in the vendor's contract, not ours; every amber service is optional, behind a feature flag, and its provenance is disclosed to clients; a client receives amber data only if its contract accepts vendor data (`clients.accepts_amber`, off by default and always off for government clients), and a client's own properties are read through a vendor only in ADR-0021's automatic fallback, of the one source or of its route (ADR-0052, ADR-0021). RED = our own collection in breach of terms: not built. There is no green route to third-party TikTok content, no green comments on third-party Telegram channels, no green Facebook Groups, no green keyword search of Facebook posts, no clean Google search route. In data the route is lower-case `green` or `amber`; "RED" is prose for what is never built (ADR-0070).

## Naming, repository, deployment

- Service names are `<source>-<action>` (per-source) or `<action>` (shared). Prefixes: fb, ig, tt, x, li, tg, yt, news, web; shared services have no prefix. The one listed exception is search-hit-router, listed under Web, which routes results of several platforms (ADR-0070).
- One repository, one shared SDK (`listening-sdk`): adapter contract, topic schemas, idempotency helpers, control-plane client, quota and canary hooks, structured logging, metrics. Schemas are written in Zod 4, exported as JSON Schema (draft 2020-12) and generated as Pydantic v2 models, with conformance tests in both languages (ADR-0030). One container image per service, deployed on "the cluster", which is k3s on Hetzner (ADR-0027). Each service scales on its own queue depth or partition lag. Deployment configuration by environment variables; feature flags, vendor overrides, `X_PLAN` and the public-account follower threshold are rows of the `feature_flags` table, read by the SDK at the start of every job, the environment variable counting only where the table has no row (local and test runs) (ADR-0050, ADR-0010). Secrets from the control plane's vault (Supabase Vault). No service holds platform accounts; official API tokens are per client (Meta, LinkedIn, TikTok Display) or per company app (X, YouTube), live in `credentials` and are injected per job by the SDK's credential client (ADR-0016).
- Language: Node (TypeScript) for every service except the analysis workers (Python, GPU pool or model API), the news extractor (Python, trafilatura) and lang-dialect-id (Python, CAMeL Tools and KLPT; the fold also in TypeScript, both checked against F7's golden corpus) (ADR-0028).
- Event bus: Redpanda from day one. Clients: `@confluentinc/kafka-javascript` in the Node SDK and `confluent-kafka` in the Python SDK, both on librdkafka, both with `partitioner = murmur2_random`, `enable.idempotence = true` and `acks = all` (ADR-0026).
  - Message metadata: every message carries `schema: "<topic>/v<n>"` (every job `schema: "job/v<n>"`), `message_id` (a ULID, deterministic where a replay must reproduce it), `produced_at` and `producer {service, version, job_id}`, all four stamped by the SDK. A change that only adds an optional field stays in its version; anything else is a new version, published beside the old one for a dual-publish window that F2's `VERSIONING.md` sets; a consumer skips a known version it does not read and parks only a version the contracts package does not know (ADR-0002).
  - Provenance: every data output, meaning every message that carries or derives from a platform fetch, carries one `provenance {route, vendor, service, fetched_at}` object (the service that fetched and when) and a top-level `retention_class`; the SDK copies both from the input record to every derived message. Data messages: `raw.items`, `items.normalized`, `item.hits`, `discovery.hits`, `poster.profiles`, `item.metrics`, `items.analysis`, `article.urls`, `search.results`, `deletions`, `news.dedup`, `crawl.policies`, and the `registry.decisions` that derive from a fetch. Control messages carry `producer` only: the job queues, `jobs.completed` and `source.events`. An author-, source- or client-scope deletion carries the signal's provenance and no `retention_class`, the one named exception (ADR-0003).
  - Partition keys: each topic and queue has one key, recorded by F2 and set by the SDK producer, never by the caller; `source_id` by default and required on every message and job about a source. Exceptions: `search.results` by `canonical_url_hash`; `crawl.policies` by `host`; `news.dedup` by `story_id`; `deletions` by `author_ref` (author scope) or `client_id` (client scope); `registry.decisions` by `candidate_key` about a candidate, `source_id` about a source, `platform` for a route-level `health_change`; `discovery.hits` and `poster.profiles` by `candidate_key` (an ordinary individual's answer on `poster.profiles` by `author_ref`, ADR-0033), as are `raw.items` records about a candidate not yet a source; `jobs.completed` by the key of the job it reports; the eight resolver queues and `jobs.poster-resolver` by `candidate_key`; `jobs.news-robots-checker` by `host`; `rematch` by `client_id`; `replay`, `rerun`, `recompute` and `retention_sweep` by their request or run id (ADR-0004, ADR-0031, ADR-0033).
  - Topics:
    - `raw.items`: every record as a fetch or push returned it, minus identities, plus envelope (ADR-0010). One nested `{envelope, payload}` message for every producer, the envelope built by the SDK: always `platform`, `kind`, `provenance`, `retention_class`, `client_ids`, `job_id`, `attempt`, `job_kind`, `api_version`, `raw_ref`; by condition `source_id` (or `candidate_key`), `platform_id` and `idempotency_key` for item kinds, `parent_id` and `root_id` on comments; optional a typed `context` declared per producer, and `redacted_fields` on any record, the payload paths an adapter removed or replaced (ADR-0009). `job_kind` says why a record was read: the job's `kind`, or `push` and `stream` for jobless receivers (ADR-0005, ADR-0009). Record kinds: five content kinds, `post`, `video`, `comment`, `article`, `result`, which normalize-item maps; four archive-only kinds, `profile`, `reaction`, `search_response`, `metrics`, which raw-archiver keeps and normalize-item acknowledges, counts and never parks (ADR-0008). News articles are an extract by rule, for copyright.
    - `items.normalized`: one schema for every platform, enriched by language and dialect detection. Mappers come from a registry in listening-sdk keyed `(service, api_version)` (ADR-0008); `job_kind` travels on it, which is how backfilled records are recognised (ADR-0005). It also carries `lang_pending` and `text_full_ref` (ADR-0070), `usage_signals` copied from the news envelope (ADR-0068), the boolean `sensitive_event` set from `screened_terms` (ADR-0059), and, for posters not yet classed, a transient `poster_platform_id` that store-writer never stores (ADR-0031).
    - `item.hits`: every keyword hit on an item, whoever the poster is: one message per item, client and keyword, with `poster {author_ref, author_type, author_source_id}`, `matched_by` (`text` or `source`), `matched_term` and `offsets` in `text_norm`. keyword-matcher is its only writer (ADR-0031).
    - `discovery.hits`: candidate sources only, in one schema keyed by `candidate_key`, with `evidence_type` (`keyword_hit`, `container`, `early_signal`, `page_search`, `web_search`, `commoncrawl`). Writers: keyword-matcher (never for the items of an X `keyword_history` read, which feed no discovery, ADR-0059); x-recent-search and tg-message-search as early signals; fb-page-search, search-hit-router and web-commoncrawl-scanner. poster-resolver deduplicates by `candidate_key` (ADR-0031).
    - `poster.profiles`: resolved identities of candidate posters and refreshes of registered sources, one flat schema built from the qualifier's ten rules, written by the eight resolvers; poster-resolver writes it only to re-emit a cached profile (ADR-0032, ADR-0033).
    - `registry.decisions`: requests to change the registry, in one schema with a closed type list (`add`, `update`, `remove_client`, `tier_change`, `dormant`, `promote`, `retire`, `push_coverage`, `health_change`, and the audit-only `queued`, `mention_only`, `reject`, `review`), from the producers F2's producer table allows per type; registry-writer is its only consumer (ADR-0013).
    - `source.events`: registry changes, written only by registry-writer from its outbox, one event per applied decision and source, carrying `previous`; types `added`, `updated`, `tier_change`, `lifecycle_change`, `health_change`, `fallback_on`, `fallback_off`, `push_coverage_change`, with a closed `reason` list (ADR-0014). Receivers subscribe a client-owned property on its `added` event (ADR-0066).
    - `items.analysis`: model outputs keyed `item_id:task:model_version` with an `input_hash`, plus `item_id`, `task`, `model_version` and `item_created_at` as fields; task names contain no colon (ADR-0023).
    - `item.metrics`: engagement counts observed over time, one flat message per read with `item_id`, the item's key, `source_id`, `created_at` and a closed `observation.label`; one writer per observation (ADR-0034).
    - `deletions`: items, authors, sources or clients to remove, in one schema with closed reasons, scopes and modes, built through the SDK (ADR-0035).
    - `article.urls`: news URLs found but not yet fetched, one flat schema with `url_key = news:url:<url_hash>` and a closed `found_via`; writers: news-feed-poller, news-sitemap-poller, news-homepage-differ, search-hit-router and web-commoncrawl-scanner's news-history lister (ADR-0036, ADR-0059).
    - `search.results`: web-search results before routing, one schema for the three engines and yt-web-search-bridge; derived data, not archived (ADR-0037).
    - `crawl.policies`: per-host crawl permissions, written by news-robots-checker with the flat column names of `crawl_policies` (ADR-0040, ADR-0070).
    - `news.dedup`: news-dedup's story verdicts, keyed by `story_id`, consumed by store-writer into `item_stories` (ADR-0022).
    - `jobs.completed`: one report per job, written only by the SDK job wrapper, a job that ends in the DLQ included, with a closed status list (`done`, `partial`, `capped`, `quota_denied`, `failed`, `skipped_flag_off`, `not_allowed`, `gone`) (ADR-0017).
    - Job queues `jobs.<service>`, and `jobs.<service>.priority` beside it for each analysis service (ADR-0023): work items for pollers, backfills, comment and reply fetches, metrics refreshes. Keyed as above, so one source is never worked twice at once.
    - Dead-letter: `dlq.<service>`.
    - There is no `raw.replay` topic: a replay is a `replay` job on `jobs.normalize-item` that reads through raw-archiver (ADR-0039).
  - Jobs: one envelope with one type field, `kind`, from a closed list whose fields F2 declares per kind: `job_id`, `kind`, `source_id` or the queue's declared key, `due_at`, `attempt`, the metadata above, and for ops and client requests `request_id` and `requested_by`; any job may carry `must_finish_by` (ADR-0057). Kinds: `rotation`, `reconciliation`, `backfill`, `keyword_history`, `comments`, `replies`, `metrics`, `first_sight`, `refresh`, `resolve`, `manual_candidate`, `first_check`, `recheck`, `ops_force`, `push`, `replay`, `rerun`, `rematch`, `recompute`, `retention_sweep`, `analyze`, `candidate_retry`. `post_ref` is the scheduler's object `{item_id, platform, platform_id, url}`, with `thread_ids` on replies jobs, except X's `replies` jobs on `jobs.x-full-archive-search`, which carry `window_start` instead (below; ADR-0060). Priority, route and vendor never travel in a job. `reason` is never the type; it says only why a job was sent (ADR-0011). An X gap `reconciliation` on `jobs.x-recent-search` carries `query`, `window_start`, `window_end`, `source_ids` and `client_ids`; a `replies` job on `jobs.x-full-archive-search` carries `window_start` (ADR-0064, ADR-0059). `push` exists only for the three receivers whose work continues after the answer (fb-client-webhook-receiver, ig-webhook-receiver, yt-pubsub-receiver): one message per pushed entry, keyed by the `source_id` the receiver finds before answering; the Telegram and LinkedIn receivers write `raw.items` directly (ADR-0011). F2 publishes a producer table, one row per queue and `kind`, which the SDK enforces; ops and client requests reach a queue only through the admin API (ADR-0012).
- Control plane: Supabase Postgres. The registry is the `sources` table. Every shared table has one writer for each row; anything a service keeps for itself is service-private: named with its owner's prefix, read by no other service, listed in F3's `TABLE-OWNERS.md` and registered in the SDK purge registry when it holds item ids, hashes or URLs (ADR-0025). ## The registry (`sources` table)

Columns every service may rely on: `source_id` (uuid; pre-allocated by the sender for a client-owned property or a news site, ADR-0066, ADR-0040), `platform` (facebook, instagram, tiktok, x, linkedin, telegram, youtube, news, web), `source_type` (page, group, account, creator, channel, company_page, site, hashtag, keyword_rule), `platform_id`, `handle`, `url`, `display_name`, `route` (`green` or `amber`), `vendor` (null, or who collects: sociavault, scrapecreators, tikhub, ensembledata, harvestapi, telemetrio, tugelbay, sovereigntaylor; on green records the licensed engine, perplexity, mojeek or gdelt, else null; for a Telegram channel the Actor that reads its posts, ADR-0051), `tier` (1, 2 or 3: the reach tier, a priority-listed source tier 1 whatever its followers; for keyword rules, hashtags and news sites the client priority or publishing rate, which sets cadence only, ADR-0049), `lifecycle` (`active`, `dormant`, `retired`, ADR-0049), `push_covered` (true only when new posts arrive by webhook, PubSubHubbub, bot or stream, ADR-0049), `retention_class`, `client_ids[]`, `owned_by_client` (bool: client-owned property with our webhook, bot or the client's authorisation), `followers`, `country_signals` (jsonb: the six booleans `iraqi_place`, `platform_country_iq`, `phone_964`, `iq_domain`, `outlet_link`, `seed_list`, plus `signal_evidence`, ADR-0033), `lang_share` (jsonb: shares keyed by lang-dialect-id's labels `ar`, `ckb`, `en`, `mixed`, `other`, `und`, plus `ar_iraqi` and `sample`; null below the minimum sample, ADR-0033), `platform_meta` (jsonb typed per platform; YouTube `uploads_playlist_id`, a hashtag's bound Instagram account, ADR-0065, ADR-0013), `added_by` (qualifier, client, ops), `added_at`, `last_hit_at`, `last_polled_at` and `next_poll_at` (a summary for the admin view, written by the primary poller; no scheduler selects on them, ADR-0015), `backfill_status` (pending, running, done, capped; `pending` is the column default, ADR-0020), `health` (ok, degraded, fallback, blocked) with `health_reason`, `health_set_by` and `health_changed_at` (ADR-0016), `notes`.

Owners (ADR-0013): registry-writer alone writes the identity and policy columns (those above but the operational ones) by applying `registry.decisions`; the operational columns have one owner each, written through the SDK: the rotation summary by the source type's primary poller, named in the cadence table (ADR-0015, ADR-0049), `backfill_status` by backfill-orchestrator (ADR-0020), `last_hit_at` by keyword-matcher. A keyword-rule source is one row per (`platform`, `route`, `query_key`), `platform_id = <route>:<query_key>`, `client_ids` every client with an enabled keyword of that query, kept by registry-writer from the client portal's saves (ADR-0044).

Per source × service cursor rows in `cursors`, unique on (`service`, `source_id`, `scope_key`), nulls not distinct: `source_id` null for service-level rows; `scope_key` empty for the plain per-source row, else a hashtag edge, an X rule id, a retention class, `reconcile`, a post (`post:<item_id>`) or a run (`replay:<version>`, `recompute:<job_id>`, `rerun:<model_version>`, `rematch:<job_id>`); columns `cursor` (opaque read position), `next_due_at` and `last_started_at` (typed, indexed), `last_success_at`, `last_error`, `consecutive_errors`, `state` (jsonb, private to the owning service), `updated_at`. `service` is the name in the service index, never suffixed. A service writes only its own rows, through the SDK helper, after the producer acknowledges; another service's rows are never an interface (ADR-0041).

## Rotation policy (decided 6 Oct 2026: tiered by reach)

Freshness is a product promise: every registered page, group, account, channel, company page and site is re-checked for new posts on a rotation, and every post's comments are re-checked on a schedule, so the data is never older than the tier allows.

Posts (pollers). One cadence table per source type, read by F5's scheduler helper, sets every interval (ADR-0049):

| Source type | Cadence |
|---|---|
| Reach tier 1 (100,000 or more followers, or on a client's priority list, `client_sources.priority`, ADR-0043) | every 60 minutes; maximum staleness 1 hour |
| Reach tier 2 (10,000 to 99,999 followers) | every 6 hours; maximum staleness 6 hours |
| Reach tier 3 (below 10,000) | every 24 hours; maximum staleness 24 hours |
| `push_covered` (webhooks, PubSubHubbub, a bot or the X stream deliver new posts) | no polling for new posts; one reconciliation read a day. A Telegram bot channel's daily job is the receiver's check that the bot is still an administrator, since the Bot API has no history method |
| `owned_by_client`, polled: TikTok Display accounts | hourly |
| `owned_by_client`, polled: LinkedIn client pages | every 30 to 60 minutes |
| A YouTube channel whose own subscription failed | its reach tier's cadence (ADR-0065) |
| `lifecycle = dormant` (no post in 30 days) | weekly; a new post sends `promote` and the source returns to its reach tier |
| `lifecycle = retired` (no hits and no client interest for 180 days) | not polled; stays in the registry |
| Keyword rules and hashtags | as each searcher's PRD section 5.1 states for the rule's tier (client priority); observed volume picks the row within a tier, its thresholds set in the pilot |
| News sites | per poller: feeds every 5 to 15 minutes for hot sites and hourly for the rest, news sitemaps hourly, homepage diff hourly; publishing rate or client priority picks the row |

- A client-owned Instagram account is not `push_covered` (its webhooks announce comments and mentions, not posts) and is polled at its tier's cadence (ADR-0049).
- Mechanics: each rotating service runs its own leader-elected scheduler (Postgres advisory lock), which keeps its due time per source in its own `cursors` row and emits a job when it is due; jobs are ordered by `next_due_at` then by tier, so a source is never skipped twice in a row; a source's next due time is set from the START of its last run (fixed cadence, not drift); a source with no row is due at once; if a poller falls behind (lag above one interval), it polls the most-stale sources first and raises a `rotation_behind` alert (ADR-0015). On amber budget tags the SDK scheduling kit stretches intervals to `min(tier_interval × stretch_factor, 24 h)` with the factor quota-governor publishes in `budgets`, so no poller reads it (ADR-0057, ADR-0018).
- Incremental: every poll reads only what is newer than the cursor; a full re-read happens only for backfill.
- Backfill: when a source is added, the last 90 days (or the route's cap, whichever is smaller) are read once through backfill-orchestrator, the single writer of `backfill_status`, then the source joins the rotation; a slow or failed backfill ends `capped` and never holds a source out of rotation; no service backfills on its own first run (ADR-0020). The route table: Facebook Page fb-backfill, group fb-group-posts-poller, keyword rule fb-keyword-search; Instagram account ig-account-media-poller (plus ig-mentions-fetcher for a client-owned one), hashtag ig-hashtag-search, keyword rule ig-keyword-search; TikTok creator tt-profile-videos-poller, client account tt-client-videos-fetcher, keyword rule tt-keyword-search, hashtag tt-hashtag-feed-poller; X account x-full-archive-search; X keyword rule x-full-archive-search, through an automatic `keyword_history` job of 90 days at priority 5 (ADR-0059); LinkedIn client page li-client-posts-poller, amber page li-company-posts-poller, keyword rule li-post-search; Telegram channel tg-channel-posts-poller, keyword set tg-message-search; YouTube channel yt-uploads-reconciler, and a term promoted to tier 1 yt-keyword-search; news site news-sitemap-poller from the site's regular sitemaps, and web-commoncrawl-scanner's listing of the host's article URLs from the Common Crawl index for the part of the 90 days the sitemaps do not reach (ADR-0059). No history (`capped` at once, `capped_reason = no_history`): Telegram own channels, web engine rules, yt-web-search-bridge rules.

Comments (comment fetchers, driven by `comment-decay-scheduler`):
- Series after a post is first seen: +1 h, +6 h, +24 h, +3 d, +7 d, then weekly until day 30. Comment steps count from first sight, so a post found late still gets its whole series; a post first seen after its profile's last step gets one `once` fetch (ADR-0060).
- Which posts open a series: posts of registered sources and client-owned properties (amber ones only for a source an accepting client watches, ADR-0052, and only with a first-sight `comments_count` above zero where given), keyword finds on green routes, and Disqus sites' articles; never an X post marked `sensitive_event`. A backfilled post gets one `once` fetch at priority 5 on green comment routes and none on amber; the posts of an X `keyword_history` read count as backfilled (ADR-0059).
- Early stop: when a fetch adds fewer than 5% new comments (and fewer than 5 absolute), the remaining series is cancelled. It is armed only once the post has 5 stored comments or its +24 h step has run; the +24 h step always runs, and a +24 h sweep that adds 20% or more reopens the cancelled steps. On LinkedIn growth is measured over the platform's count (li-own-comments-fetcher) or the fetcher's running total (li-post-comments-fetcher). Only comment-decay-scheduler applies it (ADR-0019).
- Extension: when a fetch at day 7 still adds 20% or more new comments, the series continues every 2 days until day 30.
- Hot posts: when comment velocity exceeds 100 new comments per hour, an extra fetch is inserted every hour for the next 6 hours; these extras are priority 4 (ADR-0018).
- Replies: job kind `replies` on the comment service's queue, except where a route has a dedicated replies service, which this document lists (YouTube's yt-replies-fetcher) (ADR-0070), and X, whose reply steps after +3 d are `replies` jobs on `jobs.x-full-archive-search` that read the whole conversation from `window_start` rather than `thread_ids` (ADR-0059, ADR-0060, ADR-0064). Elsewhere, one `replies` job per post, `post_ref` carrying `thread_ids` (the parent comments' platform ids, capped by a setting); no per-thread series: a thread is a candidate again whenever a step reports more replies than are stored (ADR-0060). Reply jobs exist only on the profiles the addendum lists.
- Beyond day 30: no automatic fetches; a client can request a refresh of a specific post, budget permitting, as a `comments` job with `series_step = refresh:<request_id>` (ADR-0011).
- Each fetch pages until it reaches comments older than the newest one already stored (newest-first APIs) or until the page token is exhausted (oldest-first APIs); the SDK job wrapper reports `new_count`, `seen_count`, `pages`, `cost_units` and `reply_candidates` on `jobs.completed` (ADR-0017).
- Edits and deletions: on refetch, the fetcher compares the comments with the stored set through the SDK helper over `comment_state` (ADR-0046), using one content hash (ADR-0009). Where the platform gives comment ids, changed text is a new version under the same key; where it gives none, it is a new comment, and the old key a deletion. A missing comment or post becomes a `deletions` message with reason `platform_sync` only after a confirmed second miss or a platform deletion signal (a webhook `remove`, X compliance, a 404 on the item's own endpoint where the platform documents it as deleted). Routes that never delete on absence, as their reads are not complete listings: X replies (x-compliance-sync is the deletion source), Instagram vendor comments, li-company-posts-poller's vendor posts, keyword and hashtag searches. li-client-posts-poller's daily reconciliation reads back over the class's retention period, six weeks, or six months if counsel confirms (ADR-0062, ADR-0055).

Metrics (likes, shares, views, comment counts) are refreshed at +24 h and +7 d, counted from the post's creation time on the platform, on Facebook Pages, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube; a step already past at first sight is skipped (ADR-0034, ADR-0060). X, LinkedIn, Telegram, news and Facebook groups record counts at first sight only in v1 (ADR-0058). Each read gives one observation from one writer: normalize-item for reads whose records become items, labelled from `job_kind`; the reading service for reads made only to observe counts (a `metrics` job, a client refresh, tt-client-videos-fetcher's own +24 h and +7 d), which writes no item record for them (ADR-0034, ADR-0024).

## Idempotency and deduplication

- `idempotency_key` = `<platform>:<kind>:<platform_id>`, stamped by the producer with the contracts package's key helper (TypeScript and Python twins, golden vectors) and recomputed and checked by normalize-item, which parks a mismatch; nothing downstream edits a key (ADR-0006). Keys split on the first two colons only. Per platform (ADR-0007): `youtube:video:<videoId>`, `youtube:comment:<id>`; `tiktok:video:<id>`, `tiktok:comment:<cid>`; `x:post:<id>` for every tweet, replies included (an X reply's item kind is `comment`); `telegram:post:<lowercase username>/<message_id>` (a channel without a username uses its chat id) and `telegram:comment:<group chat id>/<message_id>`; `linkedin:post:urn:li:activity:<id>` and `linkedin:comment:urn:li:comment:(urn:li:activity:<post id>,<comment id>)`; `instagram:post:<Graph media id>` through the shared Instagram id map (a post only a vendor has seen: `instagram:post:sc:<shortcode>`) and `instagram:comment:<id>`; Facebook unchanged; `news:article:<64 lower-case hex>` (ADR-0036); web items `web:result:<canonical_url_hash>` (ADR-0038). For every id-less comment route (Facebook comments under PPCA, id-less vendor comments): `<platform>:comment:<post_id>:<sha256(created_time + text)>` (ADR-0009).
- `item_id = uuid_v5(ns_items, idempotency_key)`, `ns_items` one constant frozen in the contracts package; keys are the UTF-8 bytes of their NFC form, platform and kind in lower case, hashes SHA-256 over NFC text in 64 lower-case hex, times UTC `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ`, structured data as canonical JSON (RFC 8785) (ADR-0006). `client_id` and `keyword_id` are UUIDs.
- `raw.items` is append-only; `normalize-item` deduplicates; stores are upserts keyed on `item_id`. A tombstone outranks every content version in the SDK's version formula, so a replay never brings a deleted item back (ADR-0035).
- A job carries `job_id` (a ULID, deterministic where replay needs it; a pushed record gets one at receipt) and `attempt`, and is safe to replay; a fetch that was partially written is completed by the next attempt because cursors advance only after the batch is acknowledged by Redpanda (ADR-0006).

## Retention classes

Ten years is the default lifetime of every stored item, except where a platform's terms, a vendor contract or copyright set a shorter limit, which then applies. A deletion request (a person's or a platform's, or a client's about data held only for that client) removes what it names in any class; the admin API writes a client's request, with reason `client_request` (ADR-0012, ADR-0035, ADR-0054). A record's class follows its route, except LinkedIn's, which follows its content. The classes are rows of `retention_classes` (ADR-0045), and counsel confirms each reading before production (ADR-0054, ADR-0055, ADR-0056).

- `meta_on_request`: Meta Platform Data, kept until a deletion request, at most ten years: deleted on Meta's request, on the person's request and at the offboarding of the last client it is held for (ADR-0054, ADR-0053).
- `vendor_agreed`: data bought from a vendor (LinkedIn's excepted), per the vendor contract and our author notice: ten years where the contract allows it, otherwise the contract's limit (ADR-0054).
- `x_24h_sync`: X content, ten years, with X deletions mirrored within 24 hours of X's compliance signal (ADR-0054, ADR-0069).
- `news_excerpt`: news excerpts and metadata, and web-search results, ten years; full text only in a 7-day cache, which applies only where full text is cached (ADR-0054, ADR-0022).
- `telegram_bot`: what our bot receives in channels and their discussion groups, ten years; deleted on the channel owner's, a member's or the client's request (reason `client_request`, ADR-0035), and at offboarding (ADR-0054).
- `tiktok_display`: a client's own TikTok videos and stats, kept while the client's authorisation lasts, within the ten years; deleted on revocation (reason `authorization_revoked`, ADR-0035), at offboarding and on TikTok's request (ADR-0054).
- `linkedin_48h`: member-authored LinkedIn content (comments, member posts, reposts of member posts) on every route, purged 48 hours after `fetched_at`; member profile data is never stored (ADR-0055).
- `linkedin_org`: LinkedIn organisation posts and their counts on every route, six weeks after `fetched_at`, or six months if counsel confirms that our access counts as authenticated (ADR-0055).
- `youtube_30d_text`: YouTube comment text, video titles and descriptions and channel profile text are refreshed or deleted after 30 days, a refresh being a new version that resets the clock; item rows and item-level derived rows expire 36 months after the item's creation (ADR-0056, ADR-0065).
- Derived data: item-level analysis scores, hits and metrics time series follow their item's class; aggregates and rollups keep ten years, except rollups at the level of one YouTube channel, which keep 36 months; rollups across channels of different owners exist only under YouTube's carve-out (ADR-0056).
- Archive files follow their items' class: an envelope-only archive file goes with its item, at 36 months for YouTube and on deletion for `meta_on_request` (ADR-0069).
- Purge lists: the SDK holds one field list per class, used by every executor (store-writer's column TTL, deletion-propagator's `purge_text`, yt-text-purger). For `youtube_30d_text`: `text`, `title`, `text_norm`, `hashtags`, `at_mentions`, `links`, `author_ref`; for the other classes with a content clock, store-writer's full list (ADR-0047).

## Quotas, budgets and the quota governor

Every service that calls an external API or vendor declares a `budget_tag` and asks `quota-governor` for allowance before a batch; the governor keeps the counters in `budgets` and is their only writer, services reporting usage (and provider headers) through the SDK `report`, never writing `budgets` (ADR-0042). It returns allow / wait-until / deny, which are never failures (ADR-0057):
- `deny`: no call; the job ends `quota_denied`, never an attempt or a DLQ entry, and its producer retries: rotation sets the next due time from the denied run's start; comment-decay-scheduler holds the step until the tag's mode admits it, so the +24 h step is never dropped; a backfill ends `capped` with `capped_reason = budget`; yt-text-purger deletes; poster-resolver keeps the candidate pending. `flag_off` ends `skipped_flag_off` (ADR-0050).
- `wait-until`: the job is requeued for that time with `attempt` unchanged; no worker sleeps. A wait past the job's `must_finish_by` counts as a deny.
- The SDK `report` carries an error class: `quota_exceeded` (YouTube's `quotaExceeded`, or a 403 whose reason is quota) sets `youtube_data_api` to `exhausted` until the provider's reset; `out_of_credits` sets the vendor tag to `exhausted` until an audited ops raise.

Priorities, derived only by the SDK helper from the job's kind and the source, never sent by a caller (ADR-0018):

| Priority | Work |
|---|---|
| 1 | tier-1 rotation, every client request (refreshes, of backfilled posts too, and history reads), `ops_force`, canaries, `first_sight` jobs from live discovery on any tier, X compliance jobs (never gated) |
| 2 | tier-2 rotation, client keyword and hashtag searches, comment steps to +24 h, +24 h metrics |
| 3 | tier-3 and dormant rotation, later comment steps (X reply steps after +3 d included), replies, +7 d metrics, the 30-day YouTube text refresh |
| 4 | hot-post extras, resolvers and discovery lookups |
| 5 | backfill, first sight of backfilled ids, X history reads nobody asked for (the automatic keyword history), the news history |

Modes, on every tag, green ones included: `normal`; `stretch` from 80% of a budget, admitting priorities 1 to 3, from 95% only 1 and 2; `exhausted` at 100%. Interval stretching applies to amber tags only, never beyond 24 hours. The YouTube `reserve` bucket serves priority 1 only. A client request carries the one label `refresh:<request_id>` (ADR-0018).

Configuration lives in `budget_config` (limits, shares, per-source caps, per-job and per-run caps, rate ceilings, YouTube backfill and refresh allowances), written by ops through the audited admin console; the X read ledger (`x_read_ledger`: posts as bare ids, users as the SDK's keyed reference) and the Instagram hashtag ledger (`ig_hashtag_ledger`) are tables written by the governor (ADR-0042).

## Error handling, canaries and fallback

- HTTP 429 and vendor rate-limit responses: exponential backoff with jitter starting at 30 s, max 15 min, then the job returns to the queue with `attempt + 1`. Attempts count provider failures only (429 and vendor rate limits after backoff, 5xx, timeouts), from 1; the fifth failed attempt goes to `dlq.<service>` and an alert fires. Quota answers and error 80001 are never attempts (ADR-0057).
- HTTP 401 and 403 are classified by reason first (ADR-0021). A quota reason goes to quota-governor as `quota_exceeded` and the job waits for the reset; a 403 about one item (comments disabled, a private video) ends that item's series only. An authorisation 401 or 403 on a client token or company app key marks that credential `revoked` (after one refresh attempt where the platform allows) and stops the batch, and every source only that credential could read becomes `blocked`; an authorisation 403 that concerns one source only makes that source `blocked`. A vendor key or plan error marks the vendor key `degraded`. No service ever rotates accounts or IPs to get around a block, and no fetcher sets a route state.
- Automatic fallback (ADR-0021): when registry-writer applies a source-level `blocked`, it moves the source to `fallback` instead, so its posts are read through the amber route, wherever an amber route reads that source type (declared in `canary_targets`), that route's flag is not `off`, no government client watches the source, and a watching client accepts amber; otherwise the source stays `blocked`. The alternate's scheduler also selects a green source while its `health = fallback`, beside its own amber rows, as ig-keyword-search does for a green hashtag (ADR-0021). A client-owned property falls back under the same conditions (a TikTok account through tt-profile-videos-poller, a public Telegram channel through tg-channel-posts-poller, a LinkedIn page through li-company-posts-poller only if LI0 finds the activity URN on the green API, ADR-0007), and its owner receives the vendor's records only if its contract accepts amber. Every automatic fallback, of one source or of a route, and every source that is blocked and cannot fall back, sends ops an n8n notice, never an approval card; for a client-owned property, blocked or fallen back, the client gets a missing-grant card so it can grant access again.
- Route-wide states (`degraded`, `fallback`, back to `ok`, and `blocked` when every target and token returns 401 or 403) come only from source-health-canary, from evidence across targets, applied by registry-writer, never moving a government-watched green source to a vendor; while a route is in `fallback`, its amber reads still take only the sources a client that accepts amber watches (ADR-0021, ADR-0016, ADR-0052). "Empty 200" (a 200 response with no data where data is expected) is counted per route; above 5% in 15 minutes the canary sets the route `degraded` and, where an amber or alternate route exists and its flag is not `off`, `fallback`. `fallback` is a `sources.health` value, announced as `fallback_on` and `fallback_off` events, never a flag; while it lasts, readers use the alternate vendor in the vendor rows of `credentials`, in the fallback order the canary switches (ADR-0050, ADR-0051). The canary also reads the push receivers' heartbeats (ADR-0016). A fallback also ends when its alternate's flag is turned `off`: a source returns to `blocked` and a route to `degraded` (ADR-0021). Turning the flag back on resumes them wherever ADR-0021's conditions hold; the audited admin API sends the source-level decisions, reasons `flag_off` and `flag_on` (ADR-0021, ADR-0016).
- Schema change (unknown shape): the raw payload is still archived; the normalizer raises `schema_unknown` and parks the batch for review. Archive-only record kinds are acknowledged, never parked (ADR-0008); tt-video-stats-refresher writes no raw record and parks an unknown reading itself (ADR-0010).
- Every service exposes `/healthz`, `/metrics` (Prometheus), structured JSON logs with `job_id`, `source_id`, `route`, `vendor`.

## Observability and SLOs

Per service: `items_fetched_total`, `items_new_total`, `jobs_total{status}`, `fetch_latency_seconds`, `rotation_lag_seconds` (now minus `next_due_at` for the most overdue source, read from the service's own `cursors` rows, ADR-0015), `staleness_seconds_p95` (age of the newest item per source at poll time), `cost_units_total`, `quota_denied_total`, `dlq_total`. SLOs: rotation lag below one tier interval for 99% of sources per day; comment series completed on time for 95% of posts; zero jobs lost (DLQ reviewed daily).

## Security and compliance in every service

- No account pools, no CAPTCHA solving, no residential proxies on platform APIs; proxies only on the news crawler for Cloudflare-challenged hosts.
- Government clients: X data only under an Enterprise plan with the end user declared; no surveillance, sensitive-event monitoring or sensitive-attribute profiling; Meta data never processed for law-enforcement or national-security purposes; amber data excluded from government contracts. A government-watched green source never moves to an amber route, even when a client owns it; registry-writer enforces it (ADR-0021).
- The X plan gate covers every client: `X_PLAN` (`pay_per_use` or `enterprise`) in `feature_flags` and `clients.x_end_user_declared`. Before Enterprise, X data reaches only clients declared to X at use-case review; a government client only when `X_PLAN = enterprise` and it is declared. Every X content reader drops gate-closed clients, and quota-governor denies an X request whose source has only gate-closed clients (ADR-0050, ADR-0053).
- Who may see an item follows the grant it was fetched under: `owner` (a client's own grant: Page or Instagram account token and webhooks, LinkedIn Community Management, TikTok Display, our bot in the client's own channel) to that client only; `watchers` (Meta data under PPCA or Instagram Public Content Access) to the clients in the source's `client_ids`; `pool` (our own licences and the open web: X once Enterprise, YouTube, news, web search, Telegram channels a non-client owner opened) to every client whose keywords match; amber to the pool, limited to clients that accept amber. Each producing service declares its value in the contracts package (ADR-0053).
- Amber data: a client receives it only if `clients.accepts_amber` (off by default, always off for government clients); amber schedulers select a source only if an accepting client watches it, amber jobs re-check at run time, amber records list only accepting clients; a client's own properties are read through a vendor only in ADR-0021's automatic fallback, never for reconciliation, gap fill or pre-join history (ADR-0052, ADR-0021).
- Private individuals are never profiled, listed or backfilled (ADR-0010). Every author is one keyed reference, `author_ref = HMAC-SHA256(AUTHOR_HASH_KEY, "<platform>:<author platform id>")`, 64 lower-case hex, one SDK function in both languages with golden vectors, the key in Vault. The adapter replaces every commenter's, replier's, reactor's and member's identity before the first write, on every route; normalize-item passes an edge `author_ref` through and computes it for posters whose ids are still clear; registered sources keep `author_source_id`; once a discovery poster is classed an ordinary individual, raw-archiver redacts its id from the archive. No ordinary person's clear identity is stored past classification, except on the event bus until topic retention expires. No client-facing view, export or query lists or ranks an `author_ref` that is not a public account; ordinary people appear only in anonymous segments, as counts by language, dialect, route, kind, sentiment, topic and region or governorate where known.
- Public accounts may be listed, filtered, ranked and saved by clients, and used as segments (ADR-0010): registered sources; posters poster-resolver classes as organisations, pages, channels or media; and persons' accounts that are verified or whose follower count reaches the threshold the user sets in `feature_flags` (until it is set, the follower test admits no account). One SDK function marks each resolved account `public_account`; the mark governs listing only, so a creator's or public figure's answer keeps the identity fields the qualifier needs even where its platform forbids listing it, and only an ordinary individual's answer (`account_type` `individual`, not public) is minimised (ADR-0033). Platform exceptions: TikTok lists organisation accounts only (Developer Terms forbid profiles or databases on any individual); LinkedIn lists company pages only (member data cannot be exported to customers); Instagram lists business accounts only (analytics only as aggregated, de-identified output), creator accounts read through Business Discovery pending counsel; a segment never sums YouTube figures across owners outside the carve-out; no list or segment is built or filtered on a sensitive attribute, and the X terms screen applies; Facebook lists Pages and groups. An author-scope deletion removes a public account's records, since `poster_profiles`, `public_accounts_dim` and `client_lists` are in the SDK purge registry (ADR-0035), and x-compliance-sync submits every X user id `poster_profiles` holds, beside `sources.platform_id` (ADR-0039), and carries it in the deletion target's `user_ids`, so raw-archiver's rewrite reaches the account's archived `profile` records (ADR-0035, ADR-0032).
- Every data output carries provenance (route class, vendor, service, fetch time) and `retention_class`, for the client-facing provenance statement (ADR-0003).
- Platform content in models and media (ADR-0068): evaluation sets may hold de-identified platform items from the classes a frozen split can respect (never `linkedin_48h` or `linkedin_org`; YouTube comment text only to day 30; X only with deletions applied within 24 hours), registered in the purge registry, the committed splits holding ids and labels only. Training uses licensed, public, synthetic or annotator-written data, and platform, vendor, client-owned, news and Telegram content unless the register of permitted uses (`permitted_uses`) marks that use "not allowed" for the item's platform, vendor or news host. A use is allowed by default; the register is seeded with a "not allowed" row for every use a platform's terms or a vendor contract already forbids (downloading YouTube video and audio), and with "not allowed" rows for training on Facebook and Instagram content, client-owned Pages and accounts included, as the user's own choice, which the user or the user's compliance owner may change (ADR-0068); and `ai-train = no` always excludes a host. Content Signals: `search = no` means no article fetch and no excerpt stored; `ai-input = no` keeps the host's text out of model analysis while keyword matching runs. Images, audio and video are downloaded unless the register marks that download "not allowed"; YouTube stays thumbnails only unless an entry naming YouTube's written permission allows more; speech-to-text and frame OCR run on media downloaded under the register.
- Deletions and audits, defaults confirmed by counsel before G4 (ADR-0069): an author's request deletes what it names, and retires the author's registered account (reason `owner_request`, never re-qualified) only if asked; identity is checked through a confirmation sent to the account's public contact; every deletion is engineered to X's 24 hours from a verified request or signal, on every route; a backup may live longer only if every restore re-applies the deletion log before any data is served; audit and evidence rows hold ids, hashes, reasons and timestamps only and are kept for the life of the client contract until counsel sets a period; client keyword terms are screened against the sensitive-events list in `screened_terms` when entered, and a flagged term is not run on X, live or history. At offboarding retention-purger deletes the data held only for that client.

## Qualifier rules (the registry logic every discovery and resolver service feeds)

1. Type. Facebook: Page or group (profiles are individuals). Instagram: business or creator account_type. TikTok: creator when followers reach 2,000 or verified. X: public figure or organization when followers reach 500, verified, or on a client watchlist (`client_lists`, ADR-0043). YouTube channels, Telegram channels, LinkedIn company pages and news domains always qualify as sources. Everything else is an individual.
2. Iraqi signals: any two of a location naming an Iraqi city or governorate (`iraqi_place` or `platform_country_iq`, counted once), a +964 number, an .iq domain or Iraqi outlet link, at least 40% Iraqi Arabic or Sorani on the last 20 posts (`lang_share` `ar_iraqi + ckb`), membership of a client seed list (`client_lists`). None: reject. One: review (ADR-0033, ADR-0043).
3. Activity: at least one post in 30 days, else dormant (weekly).
4. Spam: more than 50 posts a day with over 60% duplicate text rejects; default avatar on an account under 30 days old goes to review.
5. Route cap and budget: PPCA Pages per token, YouTube quota share, vendor monthly spend per source (the `source:<source_id>` sub-counter, ADR-0042), Instagram's 30 hashtags per business account per 7 days (ADR-0052); over the cap, new sources queue by reach and the client is told.
6. Tier by reach (1: 100k+, 2: 10k to 99,999, 3: below 10k; a source on a client's priority list is tier 1), `push_covered` for push-delivered properties, retention class by route, LinkedIn's by content (ADR-0049, ADR-0055).
7. Individuals: never sources, never profiled; the matched post is a mention with an `author_ref`; no backfill; re-qualified as a creator only when resolver numbers cross the creator threshold. An ordinary individual (`public_account` false) is never listed; an individual's account that passes the public test (verified, or at or above the follower threshold) is a public account and may be listed as the compliance section says (ADR-0010, ADR-0033).
8. Groups: a hit inside a Facebook group (amber only) adds the group as a source through a `container` candidate that fb-page-resolver resolves; the poster is qualified separately (ADR-0031, ADR-0032).
9. Decay: 90 days without hits or client interest moves a source down a tier; 180 days retires it (`lifecycle`); a priority-listed source is exempt while listed; rejected candidates are remembered 180 days (ADR-0049, ADR-0043).
10. Review: borderline cases go to an n8n review card (`n8n.card/v1`, Telegram or Slack), answered through the admin API; default after 24 hours is reject (ADR-0067).

The general rules apply on every row: early stop when a fetch adds fewer than 5% new comments and fewer than 5 absolute, armed once a post has 5 stored comments or its +24 h step has run, the +24 h step always running (ADR-0019); extension every 2 days to day 30 when a fetch at day 7 (or the last scheduled fetch, on shorter series) still adds 20% or more; an extra hourly fetch for 6 hours when velocity exceeds 100 new comments an hour; hot-post extras are priority 4, so they stop on every budget tag from 80% (ADR-0018).

### Other shared decisions

- Lanes used in PRD header lines: Discover and qualify; Fetch posts; Comments; Comments and stats; Processing; Registry; Support.
- Groups, channels and hashtags are tiered with the same thresholds as accounts, using member, subscriber or (for hashtags) the client's priority setting in place of followers; `tier` keeps numbers only (ADR-0049).
- Job queues are Redpanda topics `jobs.<service>`; jobs follow the envelope and closed kind list of the event bus section (ADR-0011).
- Every service that polls keeps its own rotation scheduler as a leader-elected loop (Postgres advisory lock) that emits due jobs, as described in fb-page-feed-poller, with the job fields and the rotation state this document defines: due times in the service's own `cursors` rows, ordered by `next_due_at` then tier, never by `sources.next_poll_at` (ADR-0001, ADR-0015). Who may emit each job is F2's producer table (ADR-0012): comment, reply and metrics jobs come from comment-decay-scheduler, except tt-client-videos-fetcher's own metrics observations, which need no job (ADR-0024), and yt-text-purger's `refresh` jobs (ADR-0065); backfill and `keyword_history` jobs come only from backfill-orchestrator (ADR-0020, ADR-0064); `resolve` from poster-resolver (ADR-0032); the named cross-service producers are listed in ADR-0012; ops and client requests come only from the admin API.
- Budget tags (canonical; use exactly these; an id placeholder always follows `:`, and a `<client_id>` is the UUID in lower-case canonical form) (ADR-0042, ADR-0006): Facebook Graph `meta_graph_pages:<client_id>`; Facebook vendor `fb_vendor`; Instagram Graph `ig_graph:<ig_user_id>`; Instagram hashtag ledger `ig_hashtag:<ig_user_id>`; Instagram vendor `ig_vendor`; TikTok vendor `tt_vendor` (one tag for all amber TikTok services, with a sub-counter and a monthly share per service); TikTok Display API `tt_display:<client_id>`; X `x_pay_per_use` (sub-counters `post_reads`, `user_reads` and the zero-price `compliance`, deduplicated per resource per UTC day through the read ledger); YouTube `youtube_data_api` (buckets `search`, `ingest`, `comments`, `reserve`, named after the call; resolver calls use `ingest`; no `list` bucket, ADR-0065); LinkedIn official `linkedin_cm:<client_id>` (LinkedIn confirmation calls included); LinkedIn vendor `li_vendor_<action>` (`li_vendor_post_search`, `li_vendor_org_resolver`, `li_vendor_company_posts`, `li_vendor_post_comments`); Telegram vendor `tg_telemetrio_search`, `tg_telemetrio_stats`, `tg_apify_posts`; web search `perplexity_search`, `mojeek_search`, `gdelt_doc_api`; news `news_proxy_egress`, `news_disqus`; `analysis_model_api`, seeded only if a hosted model is chosen (ADR-0023). Zero-price counting tags, never a gate: `commoncrawl`, `tg_bot_api`, `yt_pubsub_hub`. Every amber tag has the sub-counters `service:<service>` and `source:<source_id>`; a tag with a share has `service:<service>` (yt-web-search-bridge's share of `mojeek_search` and `perplexity_search`). The governor's alert is `budget_80`.
- Vendor flag values (ADR-0051): `FB_VENDOR_ROUTE` off | scrapecreators | sociavault (primary scrapecreators; the per-service override `fb-keyword-search = sociavault` while ScrapeCreators has no search); `IG_VENDOR_ROUTE` off | sociavault; `TT_VENDOR_ROUTE` off | ensembledata | tikhub (primary ensembledata); `LI_VENDOR_ROUTE` off | harvestapi; `TG_VENDOR_ROUTE` off | telemetrio (search and stats) and `TG_POSTS_ACTOR` off | tugelbay | sovereigntaylor (channel posts). Each service reads its own flag's value. The fallback order per role lives in the vendor rows of `credentials`, switched only by the canary. A weakly cleared vendor's value cannot be set until its owner is verified.
- Search output rule: a search service writes every item it finds to `raw.items`, with `source_id` = the keyword-rule or hashtag source that produced the query, which is also the partition key, so the item enters the pipeline like any polled item; searchers take the query's forms from the rule's keywords and its `client_ids` from the rule row (ADR-0004, ADR-0044). keyword-matcher is the only writer of `item.hits`, and no search service writes `item.hits`. `discovery.hits` carries candidates only: from keyword-matcher, from x-recent-search and tg-message-search as early signals, and from the services that find sources rather than items (fb-page-search, web-commoncrawl-scanner, search-hit-router); poster-resolver deduplicates by `candidate_key`, whose forms are `<platform>:<platform_id>`, `<platform>:<handle>`, `facebook:group:<id>` and `news:site:<registrable domain>`, routers mapping every other form before they emit (ADR-0031, ADR-0032). The web engines and yt-web-search-bridge archive every response to `raw.items` as `search_response` and publish `search.results`; search-hit-router routes every result, YouTube links included (a video becomes a `first_sight` job on `jobs.yt-video-details-fetcher`), writes news URLs to `article.urls`, and writes a `web:result` item to `raw.items` for a Perplexity or Mojeek result it cannot route to a platform or to the news extractor (ADR-0036, ADR-0037, ADR-0038). web-commoncrawl-scanner also writes `article.urls` for news history (ADR-0059).
